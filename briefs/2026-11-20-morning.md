## The signal

1. Anthropic mathematician claims a proof of the Riemann hypothesis, with Claude
   What happened: Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, claims he solved the Riemann hypothesis with Claude's help, The Verge reports. It's one of the Millennium Prize problems. The Verge says it would be a major result for AI in mathematics "if verified." A PDF posted on Alpöge's site, titled on Hacker News as a claimed "general proof," got 1,189 points and 770 comments.
   Why it matters: If it holds up, it's the biggest math result an AI model has helped produce. That's a big if. Nothing in the record says anyone outside the author has checked it. There's no mention of a formal proof, a journal submission, or the Clay Institute's process, and the record doesn't say what Claude actually did. In August, Anthropic's Riemann zeta lower-bound result came with a Lean proof and two internal checkers, and even that was explicitly not a proof of the hypothesis. This one needs outside review before it counts.
   Confidence: Claimed (author's own claim; not independently verified in the record)
   Sources: [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1010051/anthropic-riemann-hypothesis-millennium-prize-problem) (independent) · [Alpöge's PDF](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) (primary) · [HN thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50574968)

The headline names Gemini and Siri.
   Why it matters: Yesterday it was banks refusing to back customers who use finance agents and Meta's Muse reportedly leaking other people's data.
