2. A dead Unreal game revived with Claude Code in four hours
   What happened: Seeker Entertainment ran out of money in September, and its online game KinForge went offline because nobody was paying its AWS server bill. Co-founder Travis McGeathy pointed [[claude-code]] at the Unreal Engine 5.7 project and asked it to make the game playable without AWS. IEEE Spectrum reports that after about four hours of mostly hands-off work, he had a build where players host their own games, with peer-to-peer play over the internet and LAN. Claude Code also added persistent save states without being asked. The article says Anthropic's Fable 5.2, released Nov 9, can generate a 3D game with a custom engine. Epic Games COO Daniel Vogel says the company is "pivoting hard towards velocity" because "any fixed set of features or functionality... can eventually be replicated."
   Why it matters: This is one developer's story, not a benchmark, but it's a concrete case of an agent doing real porting work on a large commercial codebase and mostly skipping the engine's editor UI. Epic's COO saying it on the record shows engine makers are taking the threat seriously.
   Confidence: Reported (single outlet, first-person account)
   Sources: [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/epic-games-unreal-engine-ai) (independent)

3. Axiom Math formally verifies the "enormous theorem"
   What happened: Axiom Math's AI swarm finished automatically verifying the 15,000-page proof of the Classification of Finite Simple Groups on Oct 25, IEEE Spectrum reports, alongside a formal verification of the 7,500-page Stacks project. St Andrews group theorist Colva Roney-Dougal, who wasn't involved, said she had used the classification as an example of something too compute-heavy to formalize and was "really, really surprised."
   Why it matters: Formal verification checks every step of a proof by machine, and this is widely considered the longest proof humans have written. Scaling an agent swarm to it is a real step toward the article's goal of digitizing mathematics. The record doesn't say which proof assistant was used, what it cost in compute, or whether outside reviewers have checked the formalization.
   Confidence: Reported (single outlet; company-reported milestone)
   Sources: [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/enormous-theorem-ai) (independent)
