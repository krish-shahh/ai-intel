## The signal

1. Anthropic's Claude Ship lets agents approve code changes
   What happened: TechCrunch reports that Anthropic launched Claude Ship, which lets AI agents review, fix, and approve code changes. The pitch is that AI-generated code is piling up and human review has become the next bottleneck, so the new tools let agents handle routine approvals. The collected summary has no pricing, availability, or detail on which changes count as routine or what guardrails sit around agent approvals.
   Why it matters: This moves coding agents from writing code to signing off on it, which is the step most teams have kept for humans. My read is that the useful questions are how "routine" gets defined and who is accountable when an agent-approved change breaks something. The record doesn't answer either.
   Confidence: Reported (one independent outlet; no Anthropic announcement in the sweep)
   Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/) (independent)

2. Amazon buys Decart's optimization tech for a reported $7.5B
   What happened: Amazon is acquiring Decart's AI optimization technology and research team in a reported $7.5B deal, per TechCrunch, whose headline ties it to Amazon preparing to sell its Trainium chips. The summary doesn't say whether Amazon is buying the whole company, what the optimization tech does, or when Trainium sales start.
   Why it matters: If Amazon sells Trainium outside AWS, it becomes a more direct alternative to [[nvidia]] in the accelerator market, and software that makes models run well on its chips is part of what that would take. That's my inference from the headline; the record doesn't describe Amazon's plans in detail. Context from this wiki: briefs in August and September tracked reports that Anthropic was exchanging draft agreements to buy Decart for about $7B, with Nvidia also bidding.
