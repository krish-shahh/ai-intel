## The signal

1. US midterms deliver the first real electoral backlash against AI datacenter buildout
   What happened: in the Nov 3-4 midterms, local ballot measures to ban or restrict new data centers passed or led in nearly every jurisdiction where they appeared (40+ measures), Democrats retook Congress, and an NBC exit poll found 56% of voters called AI/datacenter buildout a crucial or major factor in their vote; AI-infrastructure stocks sold off the same day (Applied Digital -8.8%, CoreWeave -9.5%) while clean-energy names rallied.
   Why it matters: this is the first hard electoral evidence that datacenter siting has become a real political cost rather than scattered local NIMBY noise. Expect more zoning fights and permitting delays, which bite hardest at power/cooling and site names ([[nvidia]]'s hyperscaler customers, plus [[vertiv]], [[equinix]], [[ge-vernova]]) before it shows up in chip-vendor order books.

## Models & research

### kimi-k3-in-c
**Claim:** a portable C99 implementation runs inference on Kimi K3, a 2.78T-parameter MoE model, on a single CPU with no GPU.
**Evidence:** open repo, directly reproducible — [github/FareedKhan-dev/kimi-k3-in-c](https://github.com/FareedKhan-dev/kimi-k3-in-c), +351 stars today on GitHub trending.
**Caveat:** no throughput numbers published; single-CPU inference of a model this size is almost certainly far too slow for anything but research/curiosity use, not a production inference path.
**Worth your time:** Skim.
