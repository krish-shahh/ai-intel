## The signal

1. **Nvidia in talks to backstop $250B in OpenAI data-center financing**
   What happened: WSJ reported July 26 that Nvidia is in talks to guarantee roughly $250B in lease and debt financing for a 10-gigawatt SoftBank-built campus in southern Ohio; Nvidia is also discussing backing OpenAI chip purchases of up to $350B; total project cost exceeds $500B.
   Why it matters: If completed, this locks in years of Nvidia chip demand and marks OpenAI's first move toward owning infrastructure rather than renting from Microsoft, Amazon, and Oracle -- a structural shift in who controls frontier AI compute.
   Confidence: Reported
   Sources: [Reuters/Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/nvidia-in-talks-with-openai-to-guarantee-250-billion-financing-for-data-center-wsj-reports-4812926) (independent) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-26/nvidia-in-talks-on-250-billion-backing-for-openai-hub-wsj-says) (independent) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/nvidia-talks-openai-guarantee-250-233930971.html) (independent)

2. **EU AI Act: what actually hits August 2 vs what was delayed**
   What happened: Parliament voted June 16 (423-57) via the Digital Omnibus to push high-risk AI system obligations to December 2027 and sector-specific requirements to August 2028. What still enforces August 2: Article 50 transparency (chatbot disclosure, AI-content labeling, deepfake tagging) and GPAI enforcement powers for the European Commission. Pending: EU Council publication of the delay in the Official Journal -- expected but not yet confirmed.
   Why it matters: Prior coverage in this brief series stated high-risk obligations are binding August 2 -- that is wrong. Companies that redlined their timelines on that framing may have a 16-month runway for high-risk systems, contingent on Council publication.
   Confidence: Confirmed (Parliament vote) · Reported (Council publication pending)
   Sources: [Ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/eu-ai-act-amended-parliament-votes-to-delay-key-deadlines/) (independent) · [DigitalApplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/eu-ai-act-august-2026-transparency-obligations-agency-checklist) (independent) · [Lumenova](https://www.lumenova.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-delays-july-2026/) (independent) · [Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2026/07/the-eu-ai-act-when-does-it-become-enforceable-now/) (independent)

## Models & research

### Kimi K3 -- open-weight benchmark picture
**Claim:** At API level: #1 on Frontend Code Arena (1,679 ELO), BrowseComp (91.2%), SWE Marathon, Program Bench; #3 overall on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (57.1) behind Fable 5 (59.9) and GPT-5.6 Sol (58.9). Cost per task ~1/3 of Fable 5.
**Evidence:** API-level scores from Artificial Analysis and Arena leaderboards -- pre-weight-drop testing, so harness matches cloud serving; community weight-level testing has not happened yet (weights available <24h). Speed: 32 t/s output, 160s TTFT (very high; reflects long reasoning chains). [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/kimi-k3) · [Codersera comparison](https://codersera.com/blog/kimi-k3-benchmarks-comparison-2026/)
**Caveat:** Moonshot's coding leaderboard mixes harnesses (KimiCode, Claude Code, Codex, mini-SWE-agent), which shifts scores by 10-26 points on equivalent tasks -- the #1 coding claim requires scrutiny. The 51% hallucination rate (Artificial Analysis AA-Omniscience) is sole-source and unverified at weight level. [Kili Technology analysis](https://kili-technology.com/blog/kimi-k3s-benchmarks-and-hallucinations----what-that-tells-us-about-ai-evaluation)
**Worth your time:** Skim -- the API is accessible now and worth testing on your actual workloads, particularly code and browsing; defer weight-level judgment until community benchmarks land.
