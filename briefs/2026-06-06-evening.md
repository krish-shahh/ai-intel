Anthropic filed a confidential S-1 with the SEC on June 1. They raised $65B in a Series H just days earlier, the valuation is now pushing a trillion, and annualized revenue is somewhere north of $47B. The IPO race between OpenAI, SpaceX, and Anthropic is real and happening now. Whoever lands first sets the price anchor for every AI company that comes after.

Claude Opus 4.8, released May 28, is now leading the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 61.4, ahead of GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro. The SWE-bench Pro score of 69.2% is the number that keeps getting screenshot and shared. That is not benchmark gaming, that is a coding capability jump that shows up in practice.

[[karpathy]] has been all over my feed today. He published the writeup from his Sequoia Ascent 2026 fireside chat and then posted a thread summarizing it. The Software 3.0 framing is worth reading if you haven't yet. His argument isn't that LLMs speed things up, it's that they make entirely new product categories possible. His example: MenuGen, an app with zero classical code because the LLM is the entire compute layer. He also draws a line between "vibe coding" (anyone can build stuff now, floor goes up) and "agentic engineering" (professionals using agents while staying accountable for output). He puts December 2025 as when agents crossed the threshold from "helpful but messy" to reliably correct.

Separately, he posted that he's "never felt this much behind as a programmer." The tweet got millions of views, which probably says something about how widespread that feeling is right now. If Karpathy feels it, nobody gets to pretend they don't.

[[simonw]] has been shipping. datasette-apps and datasette-agent-micropython both landed in the last few days. He's also been making noise about "vibe engineering" as the term for what experienced developers actually do with LLMs, as opposed to vibe coding's loose, throw-things-at-the-wall flavor. The vocabulary distinction matters, mostly because it helps people in one group stop feeling defensive about being in the other.

The [[claude-code]] enterprise cost situation is a slow-motion problem that is now becoming a loud one. Microsoft is cutting Claude Code licenses across its Experiences + Devices division by June 30, routing engineers back to Copilot. Uber burned through its entire 2026 AI coding budget by April. Four months. Heavy users were hitting $500 to $2,000 per engineer per month. Nobody modeled that at the start of the year. Anthropic filing the S-1 while also launching a Partner Hub and new Labs products suggests they know the cost story needs work before it goes on a prospectus.

[[swyx]] called the PewDiePie Odysseus launch a vibe shift, and he's right. It's a self-hosted AI workspace built on [[opencode]], basically a full personal productivity suite (chat, agents, research tools, local model support) that a YouTuber vibecoded and put on GitHub. 44K stars in under a week. swyx noted that in February 2025, Soumithchintala was making exactly this prediction and people laughed. It's June 2026 and it happened. That's the part worth sitting with.

[[opencode]] itself has 162K stars and supports 75+ providers. It's infrastructure now. The PewDiePie story is interesting, but the more durable signal is that someone could build on OpenCode and ship something that spreads without writing any of the core stack.

[[steipete]] joined OpenAI in February to work on agents. OpenClaw is at 214K stars. His goal is building something his mum can actually use. Given how much of the agent space is still pointed at developers, that framing is worth keeping in mind.

[[sama]] met with Congress and Trump administration officials on June 3 and is headed to the G7 in France later this month. He's consistently pushed for the government to take equity stakes in AI companies. OpenAI hasn't announced an IPO timeline, but with Anthropic's S-1 now filed, that quiet won't hold long.
